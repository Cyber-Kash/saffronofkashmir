Our Heritage

The Story of Mongra

Deep in the Karewa plateau of Pampore - just south of Srinagar - the saffron crocus has bloomed every October for centuries. Kashmir produces around 90% of India's saffron, and Pampore is its centre. Its terroir is irreplaceable: clay-rich, well-drained soil, sharp autumn nights, and dawn mists from Dal Lake.

Our family has farmed saffron in Pampore for three generations. Saffron of Kashmir was founded in 2026 to sell that saffron direct. We sell Mongra grade exclusively - only the deep-red stigma tips, separated by hand from the yellow style.

How do I know the saffron is genuine?

Our saffron has been independently tested to ISO 3632 Category I for crocin, picrocrocin, and safranal, the three compounds behind saffron's colour, taste, and aroma, at a NABL-accredited laboratory. We send the lab report on request, and you can check the threads yourself with three simple at-home tests.

How should I store saffron?

Store saffron in an airtight tin, away from light, heat and humidity. Kept that way it does not spoil, it fades, and not evenly: aroma goes first, colour last. For full aroma, use it within 12 months of harvest. Properly stored, it keeps 2-3 years without spoiling. Those are guides, not a use-by date, and storage decides the outcome more than the calendar does.

How much saffron should I use in a dish?

Use 8-15 threads for most dishes serving 2-4 people. Always bloom them first in warm (not boiling) liquid for 10-20 minutes to draw out the colour and aroma. See our recipes for exact amounts per dish.
